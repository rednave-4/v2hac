Field Manual

You command the forces of the Republic. You do not control a single soldier — you issue orders to units on the strategic map.

1
Click a unit marker on the map to select it and open the Command Panel.
2
Choose MOVE, then click a connected location to march there.
3
Use HIDE in forests, villages, or hills to conceal a unit from the Dutch.
4
Use SCOUT to reveal nearby enemy forces and gather intelligence.
5
Terrain matters — forests and hills favor guerrilla forces; roads and cities favor the Dutch.
6
Attacking a stronger enemy in the open is dangerous. Withdraw instead of fighting a battle you cannot win.
7
When ready, click END TURN. The Dutch will then move.

Historical Context

This panel separates documented history from gameplay abstraction. FACT marks documented history. GAMEPLAY marks an interpretation made for playability.

What Happened?

FACT On 19 December 1948, Dutch forces launched the second "Police Action" (Agresi Militer Belanda II), attacking Yogyakarta — then the capital of the Republic of Indonesia. Maguwo airfield and the city center were seized, and President Soekarno, Vice President Hatta, and other Republican leaders were captured and later exiled.

FACT Despite the capture of its political leadership, the Republic's military did not surrender. Forces under General Soedirman, who was seriously ill with tuberculosis, withdrew from the city and continued organized resistance from the countryside.

Why Did the Guerrilla War Matter?

FACT Indonesian forces relied on mobility, concealment, decentralized command, and support from villages to keep resisting a materially stronger Dutch army. This demonstrated that the Republic still existed as a functioning state and armed force, even without its captured capital.

GAMEPLAY The specific unit stats, terrain bonuses, hiding mechanics, and detection rolls in this game are simplified abstractions designed to teach the logic of guerrilla warfare, not a simulation of any single documented battle.

Who Was General Soedirman?

FACT General Soedirman was the first commander-in-chief of the Indonesian armed forces. Though gravely ill, he left Yogyakarta and led guerrilla operations from the field, becoming a lasting symbol of resistance and national resolve.

What Was the 1 March 1949 General Offensive?

FACT On 1 March 1949, Indonesian forces carried out a coordinated attack on Yogyakarta, briefly retaking parts of the city — including areas around Fort Vredeburg — before withdrawing. The operation showed that the Republic and the TNI (Indonesian National Armed Forces) remained active and organized.

GAMEPLAY The "approach routes," offensive-readiness checklist, and sector-capture mechanics in this game are simplified for playability. They are not a precise recreation of the historical unit movements.

Why Did It Matter Internationally?

FACT News of continued Indonesian resistance strengthened the Republic's diplomatic position at the United Nations and undercut Dutch claims that the Republic had been eliminated, contributing to the pressure that eventually led toward negotiations and the transfer of sovereignty later in 1949.
